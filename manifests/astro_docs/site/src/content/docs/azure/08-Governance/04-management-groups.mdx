This section covers `Management Groups`, the Azure governance layer used to organize and control multiple subscriptions together.

### *Management Groups*

`Management Groups` sit above the subscription level in the Azure hierarchy.

They allow organizations to apply governance, policy, and access control across many subscriptions at once.

This is useful in environments where one subscription is not enough and Azure must be organized at enterprise scale.

<Aside type="note">
If subscriptions are the billing and workload boundary, management groups are the governance boundary above them.
</Aside>

### *Why Use Management Groups*

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Central Governance" icon="document">
    Apply policies and governance rules to many subscriptions at the same time.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Hierarchy" icon="setting">
    Organize Azure subscriptions into a structure that reflects departments, environments, or business units.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Inheritance" icon="warning">
    Policies and role assignments applied at a higher level flow down to child management groups and subscriptions.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

### *Hierarchy Structure*

Azure governance follows a parent-child model.

A simplified hierarchy is:

<Steps>
1. `Management Group`
2. `Subscription`
3. `Resource Group`
4. `Resource`
</Steps>

A management group can contain:

- subscriptions
- other management groups

This allows a multi-level governance structure.

### *Common Use Cases*

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Department Structure" icon="document">
    Group subscriptions by departments such as `Finance`, `HR`, or `Engineering`.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Environment Separation" icon="database">
    Separate `Production`, `Development`, and `Testing` subscriptions under different governance branches.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Enterprise Policy" icon="warning">
    Apply organization-wide restrictions such as approved regions, required tags, or security baselines.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

### *How It Works*

<Steps>
1. Create one or more `Management Groups`.
2. Place subscriptions into the correct group structure.
3. Assign `Azure Policy` or `RBAC` roles at the management group level.
4. Azure applies inheritance down to child scopes.
5. Subscriptions and resources follow the governance rules defined above them.
</Steps>

### *Management Groups And Governance Tools*

| Tool | Role At Management Group Scope |
| --- | --- |
| `Azure Policy` | Enforce standards across many subscriptions |
| `Azure RBAC` | Assign access to teams at a higher governance level |
| `Tags` | Support organizational metadata when combined with policy and reporting |

### *Management Groups Vs. Subscriptions*

| Scope | Best For |
| --- | --- |
| `Management Group` | Governance across many subscriptions |
| `Subscription` | Billing, quotas, and workload separation |

A subscription controls a workload boundary.

A management group controls governance across many subscriptions.

### *Summary*

- `Management Groups` sit above subscriptions in Azure
- They are used to organize and govern multiple subscriptions together
- Policies and access assignments can be inherited downward
- They are useful for enterprise-scale Azure environments
- They help centralize governance without removing workload separation
